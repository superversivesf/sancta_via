# Apple Review — Resolution Center, per-app replies (Guideline 2.1)

For each app: App Store Connect → My Apps → **app** → version page → **Resolution Center**
→ **Reply** → paste the block for that app → attach its video from
`releases/review-recs/<app>.mp4` → Send → back on the version page → **Submit for Review**.

Each block below is complete and self-contained — copy from `───` to `───`.

---

## 1 · Sancta Via Bible — attach `bible.mp4`

```text
Hello App Review,

Thank you for looking at Sancta Via Bible. The requested information is below; a screen
recording from a physical device is attached to this reply.

2. Purpose, audience, and value
Sancta Via Bible is for reading the Catholic Bible — all 73 books, including the
deuterocanonicals — with the Catholic Public Domain Version (CPDV) and the World English
Bible Catholic Edition, tied to the liturgical calendar through reading plans and daily
commentary. Audience: adult Catholics and Scripture readers who want a complete,
offline, private reading tool. Unlike a web viewer, it works with no connection, no
account, and no ads: reading position, plans, and notes stay on the device.

> Note on the family of apps: the Sancta Via suite is deliberately seven single-purpose
> apps following the "do one thing well" Unix design philosophy — each has a distinct
> feature set, audience, workflows, and icon (Bible reading, confession preparation,
> devotionals, calendar reference, novenas, prayer library, saint reference). The only
> shared elements are a small internal design system and a public-domain content
> pipeline; no two apps do the same job.

3. Setup and access
No account, no login, no configuration. Fully usable offline at first launch; all
features are available immediately.

4. External services / tools / platforms
None. The app makes no network requests. Everything is bundled: Bible translations
(CPDV by Ronald L. Conte Jr.; WEB Catholic Edition by eBible.org — both public domain),
generated liturgical-calendar data (romcal, open-source MIT), and app-authored texts.

5. Regional differences
None — content and behaviour are identical in all regions.

Design note (liturgical colours)
The app's interface colours follow the Church's liturgical colour for the day — green
(Ordinary Time), purple (Advent/Lent), white (Christmas/Easter and saints), red
(Pentecost/martyrs), rose (Gaudete/Laetare), gold (major feasts) — driven by the bundled
calendar data. The recording shows the colour of the recording day; the palette changes
automatically with the calendar.

6. Regulated industry / protected material
Not regulated. All third-party material is public domain or freely licensed with
attribution: the full list is in THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md in the public repository
(github.com/superversivesf/sancta_via) and on the in-app Acknowledgements screen.

1. Functional demonstration
The attached recording, captured on a physical iPhone, begins at the home screen, shows
launching the app, reading plans, continuing to read John 3, the reading modes, and
settings. There are no account flows to demonstrate — the app has no accounts, no
user-generated content, and nothing paid; it is designed to be fully functional offline.

App Review Information notes have been updated with this information for future
submissions. The submitted build is unchanged (1.1.63+21).

Thank you,
Jason Rennie
```
